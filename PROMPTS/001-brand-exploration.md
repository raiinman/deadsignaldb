# Brand Exploration 001

## Decision to make

What should Dead Signal DB feel like as an independent creative practice, and what identity system can hold Wakeframe, Once Human, The Signal Field, and future unrelated projects?

## Shared context

Dead Signal DB is an independent creative practice for building useful things, exploring ideas, helping people, and making software, games, tools, systems, and experiments.

Current projects:

- Wakeframe — an autonomous research system at `wakeframe.deadsignaldb.com`.
- Once Human — an evidence-driven database and build-planning project at `oncehuman.deadsignaldb.com`.

The parent brand should not force projects into one visual style. It should provide shared identity through language, metadata, project records, principles, and a public archive.

The first homepage concept is **The Signal Field**: an interactive front door where projects appear as distinct signals. It must have a useful accessible index mode and must not depend on animation, sound, or hover-only interaction.

Read these documents before answering:

- `BRAND.md`
- `WEBSITE-PLAN.md`
- `SIGNAL-FIELD.md`
- `AI-ORCHESTRATION.md`

## Shared constraints

- Do not frame Dead Signal DB as a conventional agency, SaaS company, game studio, or generic portfolio.
- Do not make the parent brand Once Human-themed.
- Do not copy Wakeframe’s exact vocabulary or interface.
- Do not use empty corporate, startup, or cyberpunk language.
- Support projects made with different technologies and different levels of polish.
- Distinguish observation, recommendation, and speculation.
- Favor a durable system over a one-page visual trick.

## Claude assignment — strategy and architecture

You are the brand-strategy and information-architecture reviewer. Develop **three genuinely distinct identity directions** for Dead Signal DB.

For each direction, provide:

1. Name for the direction.
2. One-sentence positioning.
3. Brand promise.
4. Emotional impression.
5. Voice and writing characteristics.
6. Shared vocabulary or content taxonomy.
7. How Wakeframe and Once Human would appear within it.
8. How The Signal Field would behave conceptually.
9. Strengths and risks.
10. Scores from 1–10 for distinctiveness, flexibility, clarity, authenticity, and implementation cost.

Then recommend one direction, explain what you rejected, and identify any unresolved questions that the owner must answer.

Do not choose fonts, colors, CSS, frameworks, or specific animations. Do not write generic agency copy. Focus on the parent identity and the system that supports many future projects.

## Gemini assignment — visual and interaction strategy

You are the visual-systems and multimodal interaction reviewer. Develop **three genuinely distinct visual and experiential directions** for Dead Signal DB based on the shared context.

For each direction, provide:

1. Name for the direction.
2. First five seconds of the visitor experience.
3. Spatial or interaction metaphor.
4. Visual hierarchy and layout behavior.
5. Image, motion, and interaction principles.
6. How a visitor discovers Wakeframe and Once Human.
7. How the experience works on mobile, keyboard, reduced motion, and slow connections.
8. How projects with unrelated aesthetics remain distinct.
9. Strengths, risks, and likely implementation complexity.
10. Scores from 1–10 for memorability, usability, flexibility, accessibility, and implementation cost.

Then recommend one direction and identify which visual ideas are essential versus decorative.

Do not assume WebGL, audio, generative effects, or a specific JavaScript framework. Do not copy Nullsight, Wakeframe, or any other reference site. Focus on principles and behavior that can become a real product.

## Return format

Return the analysis in Markdown with clear headings. Label each statement as one of:

- `OBSERVATION` — directly grounded in the supplied context.
- `RECOMMENDATION` — your judgment about what should be done.
- `ASSUMPTION` — something that needs confirmation.
- `RISK` — something that could damage the brand or implementation.

Do not make a final decision for the owner. The orchestrator will compare both responses and prepare the decision record.
