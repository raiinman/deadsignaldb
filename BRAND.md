# Dead Signal DB Brand Foundation

## Positioning

Dead Signal DB is an independent creative practice for building useful things, exploring ideas, helping people, and making software, games, tools, and experimental systems.

It is broad by design. It does not need to present itself as an agency, company, generic portfolio, game-only studio, or SaaS business.

## Promise

Every project is a distinct signal with its own purpose, world, and identity. Dead Signal DB is the shared source where those signals are explored, cataloged, developed, released, and remembered.

## Personality

Curious, intelligent, independent, technically capable, slightly strange, and human beneath the system language.

Avoid corporate startup language, fake institutional claims, empty cyberpunk decoration, and Once Human-specific framing at the parent level.

## Voice

Use plain language with occasional system vocabulary. Be specific about what a project is and what state it is in.

Good: “Independent software, games, and experiments in active development.”

Avoid: “We are disrupting the future of digital experiences.”

## Shared vocabulary

- **Signals** — projects and systems.
- **Transmissions** — updates, releases, and announcements.
- **Artifacts** — software, games, documents, media, and other outputs.
- **Field notes** — development observations and research.
- **Anomalies** — unusual experiments or prototypes.
- **Archive** — inactive or completed work.

Vocabulary must describe real content. Do not add labels merely for atmosphere.

## Visual direction

The parent identity should feel like a field instrument and editorial archive: dark neutral foundations, warm readable text, a controlled signal color, fine rules, restrained labels, generous space, and purposeful motion.

The parent should frame projects without flattening them. Wakeframe and Once Human may retain completely different visual identities.

## Brand architecture

Dead Signal DB is the parent brand. Each project is an independent system with its own site, repository, technology, and visual language. The portal provides shared context, status, links, and history—not a shared implementation framework.
